# How to Build a Crypto Chart Pattern Review Checklist

How to build a crypto chart pattern review checklist starts with deciding what the review must produce: a documented reason to reject a candidate, continue monitoring it, or prepare a conditional trade plan.

The checklist should make uncertainty visible. It should not turn a recognisable shape into an automatic trading decision.

This guide includes a reusable worksheet and a hypothetical example. It is a proposed documentation method, not a backtested strategy.

## Build the checklist around decisions, not indicators

A question belongs in the checklist if its answer affects what happens next.

Which indicator should I add? is less useful than:

- What information is missing?
- Which condition is still pending?
- What would make this interpretation invalid?
- Does the available entry still fit the plan?

Start with the questions below, then adapt them to your method.

## 1. Identify the exact chart

Record the exchange, pair, instrument type, timeframe and review timestamp.

If the candidate came from a crypto chart pattern scanner, keep the detection time separate from your review time. The chart may have changed between those events.

Save a screenshot showing the information available when you made the assessment.

## 2. Describe the structure before judging it

Write one sentence explaining the proposed pattern.

For example:

> Price formed two lows near the same area, separated by a rebound toward a marked neckline.

Then identify the relevant swing points and boundaries.

If the shape is ambiguous, record that. Do not repeatedly move the lines just to preserve the pattern label.

## 3. Define confirmation and invalidation separately

Confirmation describes what must happen before the candidate meets your chosen trigger.

Invalidation describes what would make you abandon the idea.

For a hypothetical close-based method:

- Confirmation: a completed candle closes above the marked neckline.
- Invalidation: a completed candle closes below the structural level specified in the plan.

These are example conditions, not universal trading rules. Choose conditions appropriate to your method and apply them consistently.

## 4. Review the opportunity available now

A pattern can remain visible after the intended entry has disappeared.

Record the current entry opportunity, invalidation, nearest opposing level and target or exit method. Include relevant costs in any later execution assessment.

Do not move the target farther away or tighten the stop without a structural reason simply to make the proposed trade look more attractive.

## 5. End with a specific outcome

Use one of four statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Incomplete | Essential information is missing |
| Rejected | A required condition fails |
| Monitoring | A specified event is pending |
| Planning | The candidate qualifies for a separate execution assessment |

Add a reason and, where relevant, a next-review trigger.

“Check later” is vague. “Review after the current hourly candle closes” is actionable.

## Hypothetical example: a review that ends with “monitor”

A possible ascending triangle has resistance marked at 100.

The reviewer requires a completed four-hour close above resistance. The active candle trades at 100.8 but has not finished.

Under that chosen rule, confirmation is pending.

The record ends:

```text
Status: Monitoring
Reason: The required candle close has not occurred.
Next review trigger: Completion of the current four-hour candle.
```

The checklist has not predicted the market. It has prevented an unfinished condition from being treated as completed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does completing the checklist make a trade safe?

No. Documentation does not remove market risk or establish a profitable strategy.

### Should every question have a yes-or-no answer?

No. Some fields collect observations. Use “unknown” or “not checked” when appropriate instead of forcing an answer.

### Should I keep rejected candidates?

Keeping them can help you inspect selection decisions rather than reviewing only executed trades.

### When should I change the checklist?

Between reviews, after identifying unclear questions or missing information. Preserve the original assessment and append later updates.

## Final principle

Learning how to build a crypto chart pattern review checklist means creating a process you can inspect and improve. Keep the evidence, the conditions and the decision distinct.
