# Chart pattern detected vs breakout confirmed: what each status means

Chart pattern detected vs breakout confirmed describes two different moments. **Detected** means software or a trader has recognised the shape of a pattern. **Confirmed** means price has actually broken out of it. Treating the first as the second is one of the most common errors in pattern trading.

## Short definitions

- **Detected:** the geometry matches a known pattern, such as a triangle or a double bottom. The pattern may still be forming.
- **Confirmed:** price has closed outside the pattern boundary on the timeframe where the setup formed. 
- **Failed:** price breaks out, then closes back inside. This is a false breakout. 

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Detected | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| What happened | Shape recognised | Boundary broken with a close |
| Direction known? | Not yet | Yes, for now |
| Can it still fail? | Yes | Yes |
| Typical use | Add to a watchlist | Review for a decision |

## Why the close matters

A candle can poke through a level and close back below it. That wick tells you something, but it isn't a close. Many guides therefore wait for a close outside the pattern on the timeframe where it formed. One set of notes on Bulkowski's work says a pattern isn't valid until price closes beyond the confirmation line.

## What can happen after confirmation

According to one guide, price will usually continue, return to the broken level, or fall back inside the pattern.  A retest can help, but strong breakouts don't always return.

## What volume can and can't tell you

Higher volume on the break shows that more activity was recorded. It can't identify who traded or guarantee continuation. 

## Worked example (invented numbers)

An ascending triangle has flat resistance at 100.

1. Price touches 99 and a scanner flags the pattern. This is **detected**.
2. A candle wicks to 101 but closes at 99.5. Still not confirmed.
3. The next candle closes at 102. A close above resistance on the formation timeframe is **confirmed**.
4. Price falls back to 99 and stays there. The breakout is now a **failure**.

A fakeout is only identified from later candles.  The wick candle in step 2 looked like a breakout in the moment.

## FAQ

**Does a detected pattern mean I should trade?** No. It means a shape was recognised.

**Is a confirmed breakout safe?** No. Failure is still possible. 

**What does failure mean in studies?** Bulkowski's break-even failure is a pattern that doesn't move more than 5% beyond its breakout.
