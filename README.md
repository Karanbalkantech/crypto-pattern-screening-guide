# How to screen crypto for bullish chart patterns

How to screen crypto for bullish chart patterns is a research-workflow question: how do you move from a large set of charts to a smaller set of candidates you can explain?

This repository provides an educational review process and a screening journal template. It is not trading software, a signal feed, or ChartScout's source code.

Maintained by the ChartScout team.

## Purpose of this guide

The workflow separates four activities:

1. Discovering candidate charts.
2. Reviewing the detected structure.
3. Monitoring selected conditions.
4. Making your own decisions.

A scanner can help with discovery. It does not remove the need to inspect the chart, understand its current state, and decide whether the information is relevant to your research.

Nothing in this guide establishes that a detected formation will produce a profitable outcome.

## 1. Define your screening session

Before examining results, record:

- The market or instrument you intend to review.
- The exchange supplying the chart.
- Your research timeframe.
- Whether you are investigating continuation or reversal structures.
- Conditions that would make a candidate unsuitable.

Keep these choices consistent during your initial review.

If you repeatedly change your timeframe or criteria whenever a different chart looks attractive, it becomes difficult to explain why one candidate was selected and another was rejected.

## 3. Explain the visible structure

Open a candidate and describe what you can actually see.

Identify the relevant highs, lows, boundaries, and timeframe. Explain why the formation deserves attention without relying entirely on its name.

If the boundaries are unclear, record that uncertainty.

Do not repeatedly redraw the structure simply to preserve a bullish interpretation. A chart that does not fit your research criteria can be rejected or revisited later.

## 4. Record the pattern's current stage

Use descriptive labels such as:

- Forming inside its boundaries.
- Testing a relevant boundary.
- Closed beyond a boundary on the review timeframe.
- Returned inside the structure.
- No longer relevant to the original review.

These labels describe observations. They are not probability scores, guarantees, or automatic buy instructions.

Keep “detected,” “forming,” and “broken out” separate in your notes. They describe different states and should not be used interchangeably.

## 5. Review context and rejection reasons

Inspect the surrounding chart before keeping a candidate.

Consider nearby resistance, the larger-timeframe structure, and recent volume on the market being reviewed.

Record reasons against your interpretation alongside reasons supporting it.

A candidate may be rejected because its structure is unclear, the surrounding chart conflicts with your requirements, or the setup no longer fits your intended timeframe.

Rejection is a legitimate result of screening. You do not need to retain a chart merely because its pattern label sounds bullish.
